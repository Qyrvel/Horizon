Boson AI has announced Higgs Realtime Duplex, a 45B mixture-of-experts model that processes audio, video, text and generated face output as simultaneous 160 ms streams instead of waiting for a turn to end, with audio tokenized at one token per chunk in and two out \(6.25 and 12.5 tokens/s\). ### [Google Research&\#x27;s Muir claims 25 MB state for 10M-token context](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1xplbuu/muir_linear_attention_with_sensitivityallocated/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A Reddit post relays a Google Research paper describing Muir, a linear-attention method that compresses long-context history into a fixed 25 MB state, rather than the KV cache the post says would reach 2.1 TB for a 1.3B model at 10M tokens. Muir&\#x27;s three claimed fixes are a matched-filter readout that equalizes slow- and fast-decaying state rows across a 100x energy imbalance \(relaxing write erasure by 17% and extending lookback horizon by 60%\), a fused rank-512 gate projection that cuts gate parameters 4.5x to fund a 30.4% wider FFN, and boundary-pivoted multiplicative decays with a recursive block-Neumann solver that avoids the fp32 underflow that NaN&\#x27;d 25% of heads at large chunk sizes. The post reports that Muir beats GDN-2 and Transformer on perplexity and zero-shot reasoning at every scale \(1.2x token efficiency vs. GDN-2\), trains 2.7x faster than Splash Attention at 32K, and reaches 90.0% zero-shot and 99.4% post-cooldown passkey accuracy at 1M tokens. These are author-reported results as summarized on Reddit; the source describes no independent benchmarks, peer review, or code release.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/CategoryNormal149 · Nov 6, 19:10

**「Why a fixed-size state matters」** Standard softmax attention keeps a key and value vector for every past token, so its KV cache grows with context length — the mechanism behind the 1.3B model&\#x27;s multi-terabyte 10M-token cache described here. Channel-wise gated delta models such as GDN-2 instead fold history into a fixed-size recurrent state, and Muir is presented as fixing three bottlenecks that the post says emerge when such models are trained over 100B tokens \(row-energy imbalance during readout, key/value write asymmetry, and unstable decay math at large chunk sizes\). The paper appears in Google&\#x27;s Hugging Face papers listing, but this item is a community summary; the underlying arXiv and Hugging Face pages were not independently checked here.

**「Impact」** If reproduced, a constant-size state changes memory planning for long-context serving: a 10M-token stream fits in 25 MB instead of a multi-terabyte KV cache, and the fp32 underflow issue is a concrete constraint for anyone implementing chunkwise-parallel linear-attention kernels. Until the paper is reviewed or code is released, teams should treat the 1.2x token-efficiency and passkey figures as vendor claims rather than measured capability.

### [Working With Designers: One Per Team, and Design Systems You&\#x27;ll Hate](https://seangoedecke.com/working-with-designers/) ⭐️ 6.0/10

rss · Sean Goedecke · Nov 7, 00:00

**「Background」** Designers, Sean Goedecke argues, are the one role at a tech company whose value is visible at a glance: a placeholder UI comes back as a mockup that is better in ways the engineer could not have imagined. They also come from a different cultural stock than the engineers, managers, and product managers he calls &quot;people of the spreadsheet&quot; — a distance he treats as a strength, since designers aren&\#x27;t tempted to make secret technical assessments of their own teams.

**「Solution」** The catch is that design doesn&\#x27;t scale by addition. Goedecke&\#x27;s worst experience involved three designers on one product: two senior replacements arrived, immediately redesigned it, forcing him to rewrite most of the frontend styles, then began reversing each other&\#x27;s changes — three or four passes per page — until he escalated to two layers of management to freeze the design about a week before launch. Both designers were excellent, he says, which is why he suspects the problem is structural: engineering disputes can appeal to latency, bugs, or lines of code, while design disputes rest on taste, and a design change anywhere is visible everywhere to users. Hence one designer per team. Design systems are the industry&\#x27;s scaling answer, and he dislikes them while conceding they beat the alternatives: the owning team optimizes for components that always look beautiful while product teams need to satisfy ugly customer requirements, so engineers hack around the system with CSS or reimplement components, and version mismatches \(a React design system applied to plain HTML and CSS\) force constant mental translation during implementation.

**「Takeaway」** Design may be the shorter leg of the engineering–product–design stool — roughly half his teams had any design access at all, and badly designed products like Azure still earn enormously — but Goedecke still wants a designer on every team, one at a time, and wishes design-system owners acknowledged how much harder they make immediate engineering work.
